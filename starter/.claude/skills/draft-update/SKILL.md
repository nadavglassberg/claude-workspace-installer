# /draft-update

Writes the message, I send it. "I" and "me" mean the user. Reply in Hebrew, short.

## Steps
1. Who and what: the client or person I named, and what the message is for (a weekly update, an answer to a question, a request, bad news). If I am answering something, find the message I am answering and read the whole thread.
2. Gather only what the message needs: the client page, the open tasks that touch it, the last exchange with them. Numbers come from a connected Toffu connector or from what I gave you. Never write a number you did not read.
3. Write it in the language the other side uses with us (look at the thread). Their name, no filler opening, the point in the first two lines, what happens next and who does it, one clear ask if there is one. Short. Plain words, no marketing language.
4. Match how I write: read 3 of my recent sent messages to that person and follow my tone and my sign-off. If the vault has writing rules under Response behavior, they win.
5. Show me the draft in the chat. Then save it as a real draft: a Gmail draft for email, or leave it in the chat for me to paste for Slack unless I ask you to post it.

## Rules
- Never send. An email becomes a draft. A Slack message is posted only after I say "send", and only the exact text I saw.
- Never promise a date, a result or a budget that is not written somewhere I can point to.
- Never put internal notes, another client's name, or a teammate's private remark into a message to a client.
- If the honest content is bad news, write it plainly and put the plan right after it.
- After I confirm I sent it, add one line to the client page: date, what was sent, what we are waiting for.
